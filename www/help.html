1. Getting Started: Registering and Logging In

The Digital Salon's home page explains what the system is for. To actually take part in a conversation, you need an account.

Registering a new account

Click Register on the home page. Fill in:

  • Full name — how you'll appear to other participants
  • Username — used to log in
  • Password — this is a low-security system; use a password you don't reuse elsewhere
  • Email — required; used to send your confirmation code
  • Background (optional) — your field, institution, or why the topic matters to you

Click Register. You'll see a confirmation dialog telling you to check your email for a six-letter code.

Confirming your email

The first time you log in after registering, you'll be prompted for the six-letter confirmation code that was emailed to you. Enter it in the Enter Confirmation Code dialog and click Confirm. If the code doesn't match, an error appears in the dialog and you can try again.

Logging in

Click Login on the home page. Enter your username or email address and your password, then click Login. If your credentials aren't recognized, an error message appears directly in the dialog so you can try again without losing your place.

Once logged in, the home page shows a Show Salons button in place of the Login/Register invitation.

If you try to open a link to a Salon without being logged in, you'll be sent back to the home page with the Login dialog already open. Logging in successfully takes you straight to the page you were trying to reach.

2. Finding, Viewing and Joining Salons

Click Show Salons (or go to /salons) to see the list of Salons. Salons are public by default, and every registered participant can see the conversations in all Salons. Salons you belong to are listed first. Each entry shows a short description; click the short description to expand the entry and see a longer description and click the salon name to open the conversation.

Opening a Salon shows its conversation: a running list of contributions from human participants and AI models, newest at the bottom. Each entry shows who made it, which model was involved (if any), any attached file, and a timestamp.

At the outset you will see a list of the last 10 contributions to that Salon, together with the identity of the participant that added it. Contributions longer than a few lines are collapsed into their first four lines by default, with a Show more link to expand them and Show less to collapse again. A contribution that arrives while you're viewing the page (rather than being loaded from history) starts out fully expanded.

The ↑ Earlier button at the top of the page will load the preceding 10 contributions; when there are no earlier contributions the button will be disabled.

Requesting membership

If you are not a participant in a Salon you cannot contribute to the discussion, although you may view all of the current contributions. Non-participants see two additional buttons at the top of the page: Join and Leave. Selecting Leave closes the Salon page and returns you to the list of available Salons.

When you select Join the system sends an email to the Salon administrator with the email address you registered, indicating your interest. You will be presented with a brief message stating:

Thank you for your interest, we'll get back to you shortly.

When the Salon administrator receives the email he or she can log in to the system and approve your request, or he or she may reach out via email to gauge your interest and suitability. Once you have been approved for membership you will receive an email stating,

Your request to join the {Salon Name} Salon has been approved! Welcome!

Membership Benefits

As a member of a Salon the Join button is replaced by a + Contribute button, and a Transcript button will now appear as well.

Adding contributions is discussed below

Occasionally, a user may want a transcript of a conversation. Any member of a Salon may request a transcript, but only the Salon administrator can actually create one. To request a transcript, select the Transcript button. When you do so an email will be sent to the Salon administrator with your request, and the UI will display a brief message stating:

Your transcript request has been forwarded to {admin}, the administrator of the {salon name} Salon.

The administrator can then Export the transcript for you. The administrator can choose to export the transcript to the Salon file repository, where it will be available to all Salon members, or send it to you privately as an email attachment. In any case, you will receive an email indicating the result of your request.

3. Setting Up Your Profile: Linking AI Models

As an approved participant you can begin participating in the Salon conversation, but before you can bring an AI model into a conversation, your account needs to be linked to one or more AI models. Without one or more linked models you will be reliant on other participants to bring an AI model into the conversation.

What is an API key, and why do I need one?

The Digital Salon provides the shared conversation space in which human participants and AI models can participate, but the AI models are provided by independent AI services. When you ask an AI model to participate, the request is made directly to that model's provider using your own API key. The key authorizes the provider to use that model on your behalf, and the provider charges you for the resulting usage according to its own pricing.

This arrangement allows Digital Salon to support models from different providers without requiring the Salon itself to maintain a subscription to every AI service. It also means that you can choose the models that are important to you and pay only for the AI services you actually consume.

A Salon administrator may sometimes provide a shared API key for a model, so you can use that model without supplying your own key. If no such enabled model is available, you need to enable a model with your own API key to ask that model to participate. You never need an API key merely to read a Salon or to make a contribution yourself.

A brief explanation of how to obtain an API key from various AI providers can be found at apikeys.html

To link your account to an AI model, from the Salon list page, click Edit User Profile and the Edit User Profile dialog will appear.

Your API Keys

This section lists the models currently linked to your account. Each row shows the model name and a masked API key. The masked key shows the first 6 characters of the actual key, but no more.

You can use the associated text field to change the API key for a model, but you cannot reveal the current key. Each participant is expected to maintain a record of his or her own API keys, as they cannot be recovered via this user interface. Be cautious when changing the key as it will replace the key in your participant record and no validation will be performed. Entering an invalid key may prevent you from interacting with that model in the future.

If you are a new user you may discover that you already have an assigned model. Frequently, when joining a Salon the Salon administrator will assign a model to you that is common to all the participants in that Salon. Typically, this is a relatively inexpensive model that the Salon administrator is willing to provide for you at his or her own expense for general use. You may replace the administrator's key with your own if you want to use the model at your own expense. The API key provided is masked, and belongs to the Salon administrator. You may replace it with your own API key, but if you do not provide a valid key for that model, you will no longer be able to engage with that model.

The radio button on the left marks your default model. The only impact of marking a model as a default is that it will appear at the top of the model list in the contribution dialog.

Click the − button to unlink a model from your account. Doing so will cause the API key associated with the model to be deleted from the database.

Add a Model

This section lists models that are already registered in the system but not yet linked to your account. Click + next to a model to add it — it moves up into Your API Keys with an empty key field, already focused, ready for you to paste in your API key.

You are not limited to a single model. Different models tend to bring different strengths to a conversation. The Salon administrator may have suggested a division of labor in the Salon's description, or you can develop your own sense of which models are most useful for the topic at hand. Most participants configure several models and select whichever suits the moment when they contribute.

Add a new model (advanced)

If the model you want isn't listed at all, expand Add a new model (advanced). This registers a brand-new model with the system so it becomes available to everyone, not just you. You'll need:

  • Model ID — the model's name as the provider identifies it (for example, a specific Claude, GPT, Gemini, or DeepSeek model name)
  • Client class — which provider integration to use
  • Endpoint — the provider's API URL
  • Files endpoint (optional) — only needed if the provider uses a separate URL for file uploads

Click Add Model to stage it, then use the + button as above to link it to your account with an API key. This is an advanced action — most participants will only ever use Add a Model above.

Nothing is saved or deleted until you click Save at the bottom of the dialog. The Save button stays disabled until you've made a change, and every linked model must have an API key filled in before you can save. Click Cancel at any time to discard your changes.

4. The Contribute Dialog

If you're a member of the Salon, a + Contribute button appears in the header. Click it to open the Contribute dialog, which you can drag by its title bar and resize from its edges.

Text — type your contribution in the large text area.

Ask a model — optionally select an AI model from the dropdown to have it respond to the conversation so far, including your new contribution. Leave this set to None to simply post your own text without prompting a model.

Models you have not yet configured with an API key do not appear in the contribution dialog. If a model is missing from the dropdown, open Edit User Profile and add a key for it.

Available Tools — expand this section to see the tools available to a model. Each tool is listed by name; click the name to read a description of what that tool does. Check the box next to any tool you want to be available to the model when composing its response. If no model is selected, tools are disabled.

Tools extend what a model can do beyond generating text from its training data. A tool might search the web for current information, retrieve a file, perform a calculation, or interact with an external service. Not every tool will be relevant to every contribution; select only those that would genuinely help the model address the question at hand.

The tools available depend on what the Digital Salon administrator has installed. The names of any tools you have selected are shown next to the section heading when you collapse the list, so you can confirm your selection at a glance before submitting.

Files — expand this section to attach one or more files already stored in the Salon to give the model (or human readers) something concrete to reference. Selected filenames are shown next to the section heading.

Click Submit in the title bar to post your contribution.

If you selected a model, the page shows a waiting cursor until the model's response arrives — this can take anywhere from a few seconds to a minute or more, and the response is delivered to everyone currently viewing the Salon, not just you. If something goes wrong before your contribution can even be processed (for example, a missing API key for the model you selected), an error dialog explains what happened and your typed text is preserved so you can fix the problem and try again.

Once a model has responded, you may see a + Respond button as well — use it to continue the conversation with the same model already selected.

5. Creating a new Salon

To create a new Salon, select Create a new Salon and fill in:

  • Salon name — required
  • Short description — shown in the Salon list
  • VCS URI, SVN username, SVN password (all optional) — only needed if the Salon's files and history are to be stored in a Subversion repository rather than locally

Click Create.

When you create a new Salon, you become the Salon administrator. Your name will be shown next to the Salon name in the header, and you will be responsible for managing its participants, AI models, Salon Instructions, and all other administrative functions. There is currently no mechanism for transferring the administrative role to another participant.

For information about these responsibilities, see the Salon Administrator's Guide.

6. Branching a Salon

Often a participant will want to take a discussion in a new direction. This is accomplished by using the Branch button in the Contribute dialog. Any participant may branch a Salon, and that participant will become an administrator of the new branch.

To branch a Salon, select + Contribute from the Salon header. Fill in the text indicating that a new branch is starting, and include any prompt that you want a model to respond to in the new branch. Select the model, tools, and files you want to include, just as though you were making a new contribution.

Once the contribution details are specified do not select the Submit button; instead select the Branch button located at the bottom left of the contribution dialog, next to the "Contribute" label. You will be presented with an input dialog to name the new salon. The new name will be the name of the original Salon with your selected suffix appended. The Salon name may not be exactly the name you entered, in case there are unacceptable characters, or a duplicate Salon already exists.

Selecting OK will cause the new Salon to be created, with a copy of all the contributions which have occurred up until that point. A message will be added to the old Salon indicating that the Salon has been branched at this point, with a link to the new branch. You will be redirected to the new branch to continue your discussion there. The new branch will also contain your contribution as well as any response from the selected model.

Contributions in the new branch may be renumbered, so a reference such as [12] in copied text may point to a different contribution in the branch. If the text area is empty the model will be asked to comment on the entire branch. If no model is selected, no branch will be created.

7. Logging Out

Click Logout on the home page to end your session. You'll need to log in again to rejoin a Salon or post a contribution.
